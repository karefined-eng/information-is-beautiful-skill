# Creative moves — the ingenuity layer

The five laws make a chart **correct**. These moves make it **memorable**. Reach for them
only *after* the standard form is honest but fails to carry the insight's weight — when the
interesting thing lives *between* charts, in a juxtaposition, a second dimension, or a
judgement the reader is asked to make. Never before correctness: an ingenious chart that
misleads is a failure, and novelty that needs a caption to decode is a failure too.

Each move below is named after a publicly known format from the field (lineage in
`sources.md`) — cited as concepts, never redrawn as spreads.

## The gate

Ask: does a standard honest form already carry the insight? If yes, ship it. If the insight
only appears when two unrelated numbers share one frame, or when the reader sees *how much we
don't know*, take a move.

## The moves

### 1 · One scale for incommensurables
Put unlike-budgeted things on a single comparable area scale — a week of military spending
next to a year of malaria funding. *When:* the story **is** the juxtaposition. *Guardrail:*
same unit, year stated per figure, differing sources owned openly, area ∝ value exactly
(this is the Billion-Dollar-o-Gram idea — same units, comparable areas).

### 2 · Time as terrain
Encode intensity as landscape: bands whose *height* is media salience, fear, or volume,
flowing across time. *When:* many small series whose shape-over-time is the story. *Guardrail:*
the vertical measure must be named on the artifact; stacked height is the data, not decoration
(the Mountains-out-of-Molehills idea).

### 3 · Grid a spectrum
Force a one-dimensional label (left/right, good/bad) into the two dimensions it actually
lives on. *When:* the single axis provably oversimplifies — both "sides" disagree internally.
*Guardrail:* axes must be defined and independently sourced, not vibes (the Left-vs-Right idea).

### 4 · Mosaic of meaning
Turn a survey or lookup table into a matrix the reader scans — categories × cultures, options ×
trade-offs. *When:* the artifact is a **reference** the reader returns to, not a single claim.
*Guardrail:* cells are the data; order rows and columns by meaning, never alphabet
(the Colours-in-Culture idea).

### 5 · The evidence grid
Make evidence-strength the primary channel: every claim sized or tiered by how sure we are.
*When:* the story is "how confident should you be?" — supplements, forecasts, studies.
*Guardrail:* state the evidence standard and its source; never grade your own claims
(the Snake-Oil-Supplements idea).

### 6 · Rank and tier
Sort by magnitude, then cut the ranking into named tiers where natural breaks exist.
*When:* the order itself is the insight, and the top is a different *world* from the bottom.
*Guardrail:* tier boundaries must be defensible, not aesthetic.

### 7 · Small multiples as story
Repeat one honest miniature across facets so the eye does the comparing. *When:* many
entities, one question — the reader's eye sweeps the grid instead of reading ten legends.
*Guardrail:* identical scales across facets, or you are lying (Tufte's lineage, restated).

### 8 · The meta-visual
Chart the knowledge itself: who claims what, how confident, funded by whom, changed since when.
*When:* the debate is the data. *Guardrail:* attribute every claim; your chart is now itself
a source — hold it to its own checklist.

## Failure modes

- Novelty without a comparison the reader can carry away — "the data exists" in fancy clothes.
- A second dimension invented to decorate rather than to resolve a real oversimplification.
- Area, height, or position encoding anything but its stated measure.
- Forgetting the hand-over contract: graphic, insight sentence, source line, checklist result —
  ingenious forms get the *same* audit as boring ones.
